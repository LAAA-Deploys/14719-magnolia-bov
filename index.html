Executive Summary

Years of Work Already Done
A rezoned Sherman Oaks lot with City-stamped plans for 12 apartments and the building permit in its final clearances
The zone change from [Q]RD1.5 to (T)[Q]R3 was approved in 2018. The density bonus plans were stamped on 04/28/2026, and the building, grading and shoring permits filed 11/05/2025 are working through their last agency sign-offs. A buyer starts at the permit counter, not the drawing board.

14719 Magnolia Boulevard is a 6,914 SF lot in Sherman Oaks, a third of a mile west of Van Nuys Boulevard and about a mile north of Ventura Boulevard. It is improved with a 1949 triplex of 2,200 SF; its three units are registered under the City's Rent Stabilization Ordinance.

The owner rezoned the lot to R3 and has carried a 12-unit, four-story apartment building over a subterranean garage through plan check under the State Density Bonus: 10 one-bedroom and 2 two-bedroom units, 2 of them Very Low Income, with 13 parking spaces. The plans are stamped and the remaining items are agency clearances. Two buyer pools will look at the site: builders who want a ready Sherman Oaks project, and affordable housing developers, who paid $183 to $194 per land SF for nearby sites in the last 18 months. We recommend a list price of $1,395,000 ($201.76 per land SF).

How to read these: Pathway A is ready now and suits a builder who wants a new Sherman Oaks building to own, including a 1031 buyer. Pathway B is how most nearby R3 land has traded in the last two years, and those buyers pay for the unit count they can fit. Marketing the site to both pools gives the most competition.
Diligence notes: Either pathway removes the triplex, which means an Ellis Act withdrawal of the three RSO units, tenant relocation payments, and replacement of the protected units under the state Housing Crisis Act; the approved plan addresses replacement through LAHD's determination. Pathway B unit counts are planning-level estimates to be confirmed by the buyer's architect and land-use counsel. Two of the three units are occupied. Relocation for those two households is a buyer cost: at the City's standard RSO amounts (07/01/2026 to 06/30/2027) it runs $11,000 to $27,400 per unit depending on tenancy length, age, disability and children, or $22,000 to $54,800 for both. If a household is lower-income (80% of area median or below), the Resident Protections Ordinance raises the standardized payment to $87,450 to $115,480 per unit, plus LAHD's relocation consultant fees. Tenants may also stay up to six months before construction starts.

The stamped plan does not work as a build-to-sell project at today's costs. The finished building is worth about $437,000 a unit, in line with the newest small buildings that have sold nearby, but it costs about $559,000 a unit to build before land. Even with the land at no cost, a builder would lose about $1.5M. To break even at the recommended price, the building would have to be built for about $150 per SF, roughly half of what new construction costs in Los Angeles.

This is the same gap every small rental project in the Valley faces, and it is why the nearby R3 land has sold to buyers who put more units on the site. It is also why we price the land from the comparable sales and the ED1 buyer pool rather than from what the 12-unit building is worth. For a buyer who builds this plan, the value of the stamped set is the two to three years of design, zone change and plan check it saves, not a development profit.

Assumptions: Unit sizes and parking from the stamped plans (14,110 SF of building area, 9 subterranean and 4 at-grade spaces). Building hard cost $325 per SF and $55,000 per subterranean space, planning-level estimates for Type III-A over a concrete garage, to be replaced by a contractor's bid. Rents are concluded asking rents from nearby new buildings (14419 Riverside, 4383 Sepulveda, Citrus Commons, Via Avanti) and LAHD's 2026 Very Low Income limit; which two units carry the covenant depends on the recorded agreement. Relocation at the City's standard maximum for two households; lower-income tenants would raise it. Construction loan at 60% of cost, 8.5% interest and a 1% fee. Exit cap 5.25%, the low end for new non-rent-controlled product in the Valley.

Key Takeaways

1
R3 sites near the subject trade at $160 to $194 per land SF. The closest sale, 5003-5007 Kester a third of a mile away, closed at $193.54 this summer. Van Nuys sites that sold with plans near permit closed at $159.91 to $183.20.
2
Plans and permits move the price. The sales at the top of the range had plans close to permit when they sold, and 12750 Moorpark sold for $364.21 the day its permit issued. This site's stamped plans and near-final permit put it with that group.
3
Two buyer pools. A builder who wants a ready 12-unit Sherman Oaks building, and affordable developers who will redesign for about 40 to 50 units. Marketing to both is what gets this site above the Van Nuys sales.
4
Two items buyers will price. Two of the three RSO units are occupied, so a buyer budgets relocation (about $22,000 to $54,800 for both at the standard amounts, more if a household is lower-income) and up to six months before demolition. The open clearances, chiefly City Planning and LAHD, set when the permit issues.

At $1,395,000 the site is priced at $201.76 per land SF, just above the closest sale, 5003-5007 Kester at $193.54 a third of a mile away, and above the Van Nuys sites that sold with plans at $159.91 to $183.20. The premium reflects what those sales did not have together: a Sherman Oaks address, a completed zone change, and City-stamped plans with the building permit in its final clearances, which saves a buyer the design, entitlement and plan check time this owner has already spent.

For an affordable developer, the price works out to about $28,000 to $35,000 per unit on a 40 to 50 unit building, in line with what Kester's buyer paid. We expect offers between $1,250,000 and $1,400,000. Buyers will account for relocation of the two occupied units, so the tenants' tenancy length and household details, along with clearing the City Planning and LAHD items before launch, are what move the price toward the top of that range. The sale is well below the Measure ULA threshold.
